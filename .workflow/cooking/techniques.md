# Cooking techniques

This document explains each technique the [recipe catalogue](recipe-catalogue.md) and [intermediate products](intermediates.md) use. The explanations are the cookbook's technique text from [B-0045](../features/B-0045/brief.md). **Matches** lists the words that identify the technique in a process step. The [cooking book](book/README.md) links each step to its technique.

## Heat in a pot

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Simmering | simmer | Cooking in liquid just below the boil, with small bubbles. Gentle heat lets flavours develop without breaking food apart. |
| Boiling | boil | Cooking in liquid at a full, rolling boil. It cooks pasta, potatoes, and eggs quickly and evenly. |
| Steaming | steam | Cooking food in the steam above boiling water. It keeps food moist and keeps its shape. |
| Braising | braise | Browning food first, then cooking it slowly in a little liquid. Long, low heat makes tough cuts tender. |
| Stewing | stew | Cooking pieces of food slowly in plenty of liquid until everything softens together. |
| Poaching | poach | Cooking gently in barely moving liquid. It keeps delicate foods like eggs, fish, and chicken tender. |
| Steeping | steep | Leaving an ingredient in hot liquid to draw out its flavour, as with tea or kombu. |
| Infusing | infuse | Drawing flavour from herbs, spices, or flowers into a liquid, often warm and off the boil. |
| Reducing | reduce | Simmering a liquid so its water evaporates, concentrating its flavour and thickening it. |
| Rendering | render | Melting fat out of meat or fat trimmings slowly, then straining it into lard, tallow, or schmaltz. |
| Scalding | scald | Dipping briefly in very hot water. It loosens feathers and bristles, or heats milk just below the boil. |
| Blanching | blanch | Plunging briefly into boiling water, then cooling. It loosens skins and sets colour. |
| Curdling | curdle | Setting milk into curds and whey with rennet, acid, or nigari. It is the first step of cheese and tofu. |
| Nixtamalizing | nixtamalize | Cooking dried corn with lye. It frees nutrients, loosens the hulls, and makes corn into dough for masa. |
| Deep-frying | deep-fry | Cooking food submerged in hot oil. Batter and coatings turn crisp while the inside cooks quickly. |
| Confit | confit | Cooking meat slowly in its own fat, which keeps it tender and preserves it. |

## Heat in a pan, oven, or grill

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Frying | fry | Cooking in a little hot fat in a pan. Medium heat browns food without burning it. |
| Stir-frying | stir-fry | Tossing small pieces of food fast in a very hot wok so they cook in moments and stay crisp. |
| Sautéing | sauté | Cooking small pieces quickly in a little fat over medium heat, moving them often. |
| Searing | sear | Browning the surface hard over high heat to build flavour before slower cooking. |
| Browning | brown | Cooking until the surface turns brown, which creates rich flavour. |
| Toasting | toast | Heating dry ingredients until they turn golden and fragrant, as with seeds, nuts, and spices. |
| Griddling | griddle | Cooking flat breads and cakes on a dry, hot pan or griddle. |
| Caramelising | caramelise | Cooking slowly until natural sugars brown and turn sweet and deep, as with onions and sugar. |
| Charring | char | Blackening the outside over high heat for a smoky taste, as with chilies and corn. |
| Baking | bake | Cooking with dry heat in an oven. It sets doughs, batters, and custards. |
| Roasting | roast | Cooking meat, vegetables, or beans in dry heat until browned outside. |
| Gratinating | gratinate | Browning a topping of cheese or breadcrumbs under strong heat. |
| Grilling | grill | Cooking over or under direct, strong heat. |
| Smoking | smoke | Exposing food to wood smoke, which flavours it and helps preserve it. |
| Burning to ash | burn | Burning wood completely until only white ash is left, for making lye. |

## Preparing by hand

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Chopping | chop | Cutting into rough pieces with a knife. |
| Slicing | slice | Cutting into thin, even pieces. |
| Dicing | dice, cube | Cutting into small, even cubes so pieces cook at the same rate. |
| Grating | grate | Rubbing against a grater into shreds or a fine pulp. |
| Shredding | shred | Cutting or tearing into thin strips. |
| Bruising | bruise | Crushing lightly to release aromas without cutting through, as with lemongrass. |
| Peeling | peel | Removing skin or shell. |
| Quartering | quarter | Cutting into four pieces. |
| Shaving | shave | Cutting very thin flakes from a hard block, as with katsuobushi. |
| Mixing | mix | Combining ingredients evenly, stirred with a spoon. |
| Kneading | knead | Working dough by pressing and folding until it turns smooth and stretchy. |
| Whisking | whisk | Beating quickly to blend, aerate, or thicken. |
| Beating | beat | Mixing hard to add air or combine thoroughly. |
| Folding | fold | Combining gently so a light mixture keeps its air. |
| Tempering | temper | Warming eggs slowly with hot liquid so they do not scramble, or setting melted chocolate so it snaps. |
| Emulsifying | emulsify | Whisking oil slowly into a liquid until they join, as in mayonnaise. |
| Marinating | marinate | Soaking food in a seasoned liquid to flavour and tenderise it. |
| Seasoning | season | Adding salt, spices, or acid to balance a dish's flavour. |
| Dipping | dip | Submerging briefly in a liquid, such as lye for pretzels or sauce for tortillas. |
| Diluting | dilute | Adding water to a syrup or concentrate to make a drink. |
| Shaping | shape | Forming dough or a mixture into its final form. |
| Rolling | roll | Flattening with a rolling pin, or rolling around a filling. |
| Filling | fill | Putting a filling into pastry, dough, or another food. |
| Wrapping | wrap | Enclosing food in dough, leaves, or husks. |
| Skewering | skewer | Threading pieces onto skewers for grilling. |
| Layering | layer | Building a dish in layers, as with lasagne and trifles. |
| Assembling | assemble, serve | Putting finished components together into a dish. |
| Pounding | pound | Crushing in a mortar and pestle to release oils and make pastes. |
| Mashing | mash | Crushing soft food into a smooth mass. |

## Separating

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Straining | strain | Pouring through a strainer to separate liquid from solids. |
| Draining | drain | Letting liquid run off solids, as whey from curds. |
| Settling | settle | Letting a mixture stand until solids sink or cream rises. |
| Leaching | through the ash | Running water slowly through ash to dissolve the lye out of it. |
| Skimming | skim | Lifting the top layer, such as cream, off a liquid. |
| Churning | churn | Agitating cream until its fat clumps into butter, leaving buttermilk. |
| Pressing | press | Squeezing out juice, oil, or whey under pressure. |

## Grinding

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Grinding | grind | Breaking food into small particles, from coarse mince to fine flour. |
| Crushing | crush | Breaking food into pieces or a pulp so it can be pressed. |
| Blending | blend, purée | Turning ingredients into a smooth liquid or paste. |
| Cracking | crack | Breaking grain or shells coarsely. |

## Butchery

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Skinning | skin | Removing the hide from a carcass before butchering. |
| Plucking | pluck | Pulling the feathers from a scalded bird. |
| Scraping | scrape | Removing bristles from a scalded pig's skin. |
| Butchering | butcher | Breaking a dressed carcass into cuts, offal, fat, and bones. |
| Filleting | fillet | Cutting fish flesh away from the bones. |
| Scaling | scale | Scraping scales from a fish before filleting. |
| Stuffing | stuff | Filling casings with seasoned meat to make sausages. |

## Time

| Technique | Matches | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Drying | dry, wither | Removing water with air or gentle warmth, which concentrates flavour and preserves food. |
| Fermenting | ferment | Letting yeasts or bacteria transform food over time, creating sourness, depth, and preservation. |
| Culturing | culture | Growing a chosen microbe, such as yogurt, koji, or a sourdough starter, in food. |
| Souring | sour | Letting bacteria turn alcohol or milk sour, as with vinegar and quark. |
| Pickling | pickle | Preserving food in vinegar or brine. |
| Curing | cure | Preserving meat or fish with salt, sometimes with smoke or drying. |
| Brining | brine | Soaking in salt water to season and preserve. |
| Ageing | age, ripen, oxidise | Leaving food for a long time so its flavour deepens, as with cheese, miso, and tea. |
| Proofing | proof | Letting yeast dough rise before baking. |
| Chilling | chill, cool | Cooling food to set it or serve it cold. |
| Resting | rest | Leaving dough or batter to relax before shaping or cooking. |
| Setting | set | Leaving food to firm up, as with jam, sugar, and chocolate. |
| Soaking | soak | Leaving dry food in water to soften it. |
| Sprouting | sprout | Letting soaked grains or beans start to grow, as in malting. |
