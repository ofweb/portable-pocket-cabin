# Ingredient catalogue

> Source material from the cooking design conversation. Only formatting has changed. Where this file differs from the [cooking concept](../concept.md), the concept states the current direction. Farmer's Delight references predate the decision to replace it with cabin cooking.

I'd start with this as our complete candidate list. It includes raw ingredients, things players grow or forage, and intermediate products made through cooking.

We should reuse ingredients from vanilla Minecraft and Farmer's Delight wherever possible. Farmer's Delight already supplies tomatoes, onions, cabbage and rice, along with several processed ingredients.

I'm including some more ambitious ingredients because we want real recipes from different cuisines, meaningful exploration, and increasingly sophisticated food preparation.

## 17. A few rules for keeping this manageable

This is already a fairly large catalogue. The important part is how we represent it.

**Ingredient families:** Recipes should be able to accept any suitable mushroom, flour, oil, stock or cut of meat, unless a specific variety matters. For example, mushroom soup might accept any edible mushroom, while shiitake dashi requires dried shiitake.

**Processing states:** Chopping, washing and mixing don't always need to create new inventory items. We should primarily create separate items when the product can be stored, traded, reused or further processed.

**Byproducts:** Buttermilk, whey, bones, fat and trimmings should have useful purposes, making different production chains connect naturally.

**Regional ingredients:** Some crops and spices should require discovering particular biomes before they can be cultivated. Rare wild mushrooms could remain forage-only.

**Reuse existing mod content:** Farmer's Delight already includes products such as wheat dough, raw pasta, minced beef, beef patties, bone broth and tomato sauce. We should integrate those rather than implement duplicate versions.

## My suggested initial scope

Even though the master list is large, I would start implementation with a few connected ingredient families:

1. **Grains and baking:** Wheat, rye, oats, flour, yeast, sourdough and butter.
2. **Vegetables and sauces:** Onion, garlic, leek, celery, carrot, tomato, herbs, chili and salt.
3. **Butchery:** Cow, pig, sheep and chicken carcasses, their cuts, mince, sausages, bones and fat.
4. **Mushrooms and stock:** Several mushroom varieties, drying, vegetable stock, meat stock and dashi.
5. **Fermentation:** Sourdough, pickled vegetables, sauerkraut, kimchi and hot sauce.

That would establish all the major processing mechanics. Then we can introduce more varieties and elaborate dishes without having to invent entirely new systems every time.

The next useful design step would be to turn this catalogue into an **ingredient dependency tree**, showing which raw resources unlock which intermediate products and ultimately which recipes. That will also reveal ingredients with too few uses and ingredients that are becoming unnecessarily important.
