# Target recipes

> Source material from the cooking design conversation. Only formatting has changed. Where this file differs from the [cooking concept](../concept.md), the concept states the current direction. Farmer's Delight references predate the decision to replace it with cabin cooking.

## Main dishes

I'd start with **8 dishes from each of the 7 cuisines**, giving us 56 recipes to design around.

The selection should cover a range of complexity and introduce different culinary techniques. I especially want dishes that reuse ingredients and intermediate products we've already discussed: flour, stock, fermented vegetables, sausages, cheese, sauces, spices and prepared meat.

I've given each recipe a suggested complexity tier:

- **Tier 1–2:** Basic cooking, simple preparation and a few ingredients.
- **Tier 3:** Several preparation steps and reusable intermediate products.
- **Tier 4:** More demanding techniques, fermentation or complex components.
- **Tier 5:** Elaborate production chains combining multiple advanced processes.

These tiers describe the complete from-scratch production chain. They aren't equipment unlock levels.

### 1. French cuisine

French food is particularly useful for teaching sauces, stocks, reductions, braising and pastry.

| Dish | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Ratatouille** | Tomatoes, aubergine, courgette, peppers, garlic and herbs. Sautéing and gentle cooking | 2 |
| **French onion soup** | Caramelised onions, beef stock, bread and cheese. Slow cooking and gratinating | 3 |
| **Quiche Lorraine** | Shortcrust pastry, eggs, cream and bacon. Pastry making and custard baking | 3 |
| **Moules marinières** | Mussels, shallots, herbs and white wine. Sweating aromatics and steaming shellfish | 3 |
| **Boeuf bourguignon** | Beef cuts, red wine, stock, mushrooms and bacon. Searing, deglazing and slow braising | 4 |
| **Coq au vin** | Chicken, red wine, bacon, mushrooms and stock. Butchery, browning and braising | 4 |
| **Tarte Tatin** | Apples, caramel, butter and pastry. Caramelisation and inverted baking | 4 |
| **Cassoulet** | Dried white beans, sausages, pork and preserved duck. Soaking, curing, slow cooking and baking | 5 |

**Standout recipes:** Boeuf bourguignon and cassoulet. Both demonstrate how ordinary ingredients can become elaborate dishes through processing and time.

### 2. German cuisine

German dishes give us excellent reasons to develop butchery, sausages, fermentation, grain milling and baking.

| Dish | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Kartoffelpuffer** | Grated potatoes, onion, flour and egg. Grating and shallow frying | 2 |
| **Spätzle** | Flour, eggs and water. Making, shaping and boiling fresh egg noodles | 2 |
| **Käsespätzle** | Homemade Spätzle, cheese and caramelised onions. Layering and melting | 3 |
| **Brezeln** | Yeast dough shaped into pretzels, dipped in alkaline solution and baked | 3 |
| **Bratwurst mit Sauerkraut** | Minced pork, fat, spices, sausage casings and fermented cabbage. Sausage making and fermentation | 4 |
| **Rinderrouladen** | Thin beef slices, mustard, bacon, onion and pickles. Rolling, searing and braising | 4 |
| **Sauerbraten** | Beef marinated in vinegar, spices and aromatics, then braised into a roast | 4 |
| **Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte** | Chocolate sponge, cherries, whipped cream and cherry syrup. Baking, whipping and layering | 5 |

**Standout recipes:** Bratwurst with sauerkraut, because the player makes both sausage and fermented cabbage, and Sauerbraten for marinating and braising.

Germany's regional food traditions provide plenty of opportunities to connect preservation, meat processing and baking.

### 3. Japanese cuisine

Japanese cooking gives us a strong reason to develop dashi, rice preparation, fermentation, fish processing and careful temperature control.

| Dish | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Onigiri** | Cooked rice, nori and fillings. Rice preparation, seasoning and shaping | 2 |
| **Miso soup** | Dashi, miso, tofu, seaweed and spring onions. Stock extraction and gentle heating | 3 |
| **Okonomiyaki** | Cabbage, flour, dashi, eggs and toppings. Preparing batter and controlled pan cooking | 3 |
| **Teriyaki salmon** | Salmon, soy sauce, mirin, sake and sugar. Pan-frying, reduction and glazing | 3 |
| **Tempura** | Vegetables or seafood, light batter and oil. Deep-frying and oil temperature control | 3 |
| **Gyoza** | Wheat wrappers, minced pork, cabbage, ginger and garlic. Filling, folding, frying and steaming | 4 |
| **Maki sushi** | Seasoned sushi rice, nori, fish or vegetables. Rice seasoning, cutting and rolling | 4 |
| **Shoyu ramen** | Meat stock, dashi, noodles, tare, braised pork, eggs and toppings. Multiple independently prepared components | 5 |

**Standout recipes:** Gyoza and ramen. Gyoza teaches dough preparation and a combination of frying and steaming, while ramen shows what can be achieved by combining several independently produced ingredients.

Dashi is especially important because it becomes a shared ingredient for numerous Japanese dishes.

### 4. Thai cuisine

Thai food could be our strongest example of balancing sweet, sour, salty, umami and chili heat. It also creates a demand for tropical herbs, spices and fruit.

| Dish | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Som tam** | Green papaya, lime, chili, garlic, fish sauce, palm sugar and peanuts. Pounding and flavour balancing | 2 |
| **Pad kra pao** | Minced meat, garlic, chili, holy basil and sauce. High-temperature stir-frying | 2 |
| **Tom kha gai** | Chicken, coconut milk, galangal, lemongrass and lime leaves. Infusing aromatics and gentle simmering | 3 |
| **Tom yum goong** | Shrimp, lemongrass, galangal, lime, chili and fish sauce. Broth making and balancing acidity | 3 |
| **Pad Thai** | Rice noodles, tamarind sauce, egg, tofu or shrimp and peanuts. Soaking noodles, stir-frying and balancing flavours | 3 |
| **Mango sticky rice** | Glutinous rice, coconut milk, sugar, salt and ripe mango. Steaming and sweet coconut preparation | 3 |
| **Green curry** | Green curry paste, coconut milk, vegetables and chicken. Grinding spices, frying paste and simmering | 4 |
| **Massaman curry** | Beef or chicken, potatoes, coconut milk, roasted spices and peanuts. Spice processing and slow braising | 4 |

**Standout recipes:** Pad Thai and green curry.

Pad Thai introduces tamarind as an important sour ingredient, while green curry creates an entire processing chain for fresh herbs, chilies and ground curry paste.

For real-world recipe references, [Pad Thai](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/best-pad-thai/) and [green curry paste](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/green-curry-paste/) are particularly useful.

### 5. Mexican cuisine

Mexican food is particularly valuable for corn processing, dried chilies, slow-cooked meat, complex sauces and steaming.

| Dish | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Quesadillas** | Corn tortillas, cheese and optional fillings. Preparing masa, pressing tortillas and cooking on a griddle | 2 |
| **Enchiladas verdes** | Corn tortillas, chicken, tomatillo salsa and cheese. Roasting or boiling vegetables, blending sauce and assembling | 3 |
| **Chiles rellenos** | Poblano chilies, cheese or meat filling, egg batter. Roasting, peeling, stuffing and frying | 3 |
| **Carnitas tacos** | Pork shoulder, spices, citrus, tortillas, onions and salsa. Slow cooking pork in fat and preparing accompaniments | 4 |
| **Tamales** | Corn masa, fat, meat or vegetable filling and corn husks. Dough preparation, filling, wrapping and steaming | 4 |
| **Pozole rojo** | Hominy, pork, dried chilies, garlic and vegetables. Corn processing, chili sauce preparation and long simmering | 4 |
| **Tacos al pastor** | Marinated pork, dried chilies, achiote, pineapple and tortillas. Marinading, roasting and assembly | 4 |
| **Mole poblano** | Several dried chilies, nuts, seeds, spices, chocolate and stock. Roasting, grinding, blending and slow cooking | 5 |

**Standout recipes:** Tamales and mole poblano.

Tamales introduce steaming wrapped dough and a very useful new intermediate ingredient: nixtamalized corn masa.

Mole poblano is a wonderful late-game target. A traditional version can involve several different chilies, toasted nuts and seeds, spices, fruit, chocolate and stock, each requiring preparation.

The [México in My Kitchen recipe collection](https://www.mexicoinmykitchen.com/recipes/) would be a useful reference for this entire branch.

### 6. South American cuisine

I'd deliberately select dishes from several countries rather than treating South America as one cuisine.

| Dish | Country | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arepas con queso** | Colombia / Venezuela | Precooked cornmeal, water, salt and cheese. Making and cooking corn cakes | 2 |
| **Pão de queijo** | Brazil | Tapioca starch, cheese, milk, eggs and oil. Starch preparation and baking | 3 |
| **Ceviche** | Peru | Fish, lime, red onion, chili, coriander and sometimes fish stock. Fish preparation and acid marination | 3 |
| **Lomo saltado** | Peru | Beef strips, onion, tomato, soy sauce, vinegar, potatoes and rice. High-heat stir-frying | 3 |
| **Empanadas de carne** | Argentina | Pastry dough, minced beef, onion, egg and olives. Making fillings, folding and baking | 4 |
| **Moqueca baiana** | Brazil | Fish, tomato, peppers, coconut milk, palm oil and lime. Layering ingredients and gentle simmering | 4 |
| **Pastel de choclo** | Chile | Fresh corn topping, minced beef, onion, eggs, olives and raisins. Preparing separate components and baking | 4 |

**Standout recipes:** Feijoada and pastel de choclo.

Feijoada is especially useful for demonstrating how different cuts of an animal can be used in one dish, including preserved meats.

Pastel de choclo is a nice combination of vegetables, meat, fruit sweetness and baking.

Ceviche also adds a distinctive technique through preparing fish with lime juice.

### 7. Danish cuisine

Danish food would connect exceptionally well with our rye, oats, pork, dairy, preservation and sauce systems.

| Dish | What the player makes and learns | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Frikadeller** | Minced pork, onion, egg, flour and milk. Grinding, mixing fars, shaping and frying | 2 |
| **Stegt flæsk med persillesovs** | Sliced pork belly, potatoes, flour, butter, milk and parsley. Frying and making white sauce | 3 |
| **Rugbrød** | Rye flour, sourdough starter, water, salt and seeds. Fermentation, proofing and baking | 3 |
| **Boller i karry** | Minced meat, eggs, curry spices, flour, milk and rice. Preparing forcemeat, poaching meatballs and thickening sauce | 3 |
| **Flæskesteg med rødkål** | Pork roast, red cabbage, potatoes, stock and pan drippings. Roasting, braising cabbage and making gravy | 4 |
| **Sildemad med karrysalat** | Rye bread, pickled herring, egg, mayonnaise, curry and onions. Pickling, emulsifying and assembling | 4 |
| **Tarteletter med høns i asparges** | Pastry shells, chicken, stock, asparagus, flour and butter. Stock making, thickening and pastry baking | 4 |
| **Risalamande med kirsebærsauce** | Rice pudding, milk, cream, almonds, vanilla and cherries. Simmering, whipping, folding and fruit sauce preparation | 4 |

**Standout recipes:** Rugbrød, tarteletter and sildemad.

Tarteletter are a surprisingly good example of the hidden complexity we're trying to show. Preparing everything from scratch requires pastry, butchery, stock making, sauce thickening and combining the final filling.

And sildemad can involve rye fermentation, fish preservation, mayonnaise emulsification and preparing curry seasoning, even though assembling it takes very little work.

### Which dishes should we build first?

I'd choose a smaller set of recipes to develop the mechanics before adding all 56.

| Recipe | Why it should come early |
|---|---|
| Ratatouille | Tests vegetable cultivation, chopping and sautéing |
| French onion soup | Caramelisation, stock, reduction and gratinating |
| Spätzle | Flour, eggs, shaping and boiling |
| Bratwurst with sauerkraut | Butchery, mincing, stuffing and fermentation |
| Miso soup | Dashi and gentle heating |
| Gyoza | Dough wrappers, meat processing, frying and steaming |
| Pad Thai | Flavour balancing, prepared sauce and noodles |
| Green curry | Growing spices and herbs, making curry paste |
| Quesadillas | Corn processing, tortillas and cheese |
| Tamales | Corn dough, filling, wrapping and steaming |
| Ceviche | Fish preparation, citrus and marination |
| Feijoada | Meat cuts, preservation, beans and long simmering |
| Rugbrød | Milling, sourdough and baking |
| Tarteletter | Pastry, chicken stock and thickened sauce |

These 14 recipes collectively exercise most of the systems we've discussed.

The **largest payoff** will come from building shared intermediate products before implementing lots of finished dishes. For example, getting flour, stock, cheese, tomato sauce, fermented vegetables, curry paste and corn masa working gives us the foundation for a large proportion of the 56 recipes.

I'd retain all 56 as our initial target catalogue, then use their dependency graphs to determine which ingredients, workstations and processes to implement first.

## Desserts and snacks

I'd add **4 desserts and 4 snacks from each of our seven cuisines**, giving us another 56 recipes. Together with the 56 main dishes, that brings our target catalogue to **112 dishes**.

Desserts will introduce techniques we haven't explored much yet: custards, caramelisation, chocolate making, whipping, pastry, confectionery and chilling.

Snacks are equally interesting. Something as simple as a sausage roll can involve butchery, sausage making, milling, fermentation, dough preparation and baking.

I'll use the same complexity scale of 1–5, based on preparing everything from scratch. The aim is to find real dishes with interesting ingredient dependencies.

### 1. French — pastry, custards and sauces

| Type | Recipe | Main ingredients and techniques | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dessert | **Madeleines** | Flour, eggs, butter, sugar, lemon zest. Whipping batter, baking | 2 |
| Dessert | **Crème brûlée** | Cream, egg yolks, vanilla, sugar. Setting custard, caramelising sugar | 3 |
| Dessert | **Profiteroles** | Choux pastry, whipped cream or pastry cream, chocolate sauce. Cooking dough, piping, baking, filling | 4 |
| Dessert | **Canelés** | Milk, butter, flour, eggs, sugar, vanilla. Resting batter, high-temperature baking, caramelising exterior | 4 |
| Snack | **Gougères** | Choux pastry, cheese, eggs. Preparing savoury choux and baking | 3 |
| Snack | **Croque monsieur** | Bread, ham, cheese, béchamel. Assembling, toasting, gratinating | 3 |
| Snack | **Socca** | Chickpea flour, water, olive oil. Grinding legumes, preparing batter, high-heat cooking | 2 |
| Snack | **Pissaladière** | Bread dough, caramelised onions, anchovies, olives. Slow cooking and baking | 4 |

French desserts are especially useful because the same choux pastry can produce profiteroles, éclairs and gougères. A good example of one technique unlocking several dishes.

**Interesting new intermediates:** Choux pastry, pastry cream, custard, chocolate sauce, caramel.

### 9. Giving desserts and snacks useful buffs

One thing I'd do differently from main meals is make **buff duration and convenience part of their identity**.

| Food category | Gameplay role |
|---|---|
| Full meals | Stronger, longer-lasting buffs |
| Savoury snacks | Moderate buffs, convenient during exploration |
| Sweet snacks | Short, useful bursts of energy or agility |
| Elaborate desserts | Specialised buffs, potentially longer than ordinary sweets |
| Preserved snacks | Portable food with useful duration or efficiency |

For example, a cheeseburger might provide longer-lasting endurance and resilience. A piece of chocolate could provide a brief mining-speed improvement. A carefully prepared dessert could give a more substantial short-term effect.

I'd also let players eat snacks while their main meal's buff is still active, with limits to prevent unlimited stacking. This makes it worthwhile for the cabin to maintain a variety of food.

---

We now have **112 candidate dishes across seven cuisines**, covering meals, desserts and snacks.

The next useful refinement is to work backwards from all 112 recipes and identify the smallest practical set of shared ingredients, intermediate products and equipment.

That's where we can start reducing unnecessary duplication while retaining the complexity that makes the farm-to-table experience interesting.

## Drinks

I'd add **35 non-alcoholic drinks**, five from each of our seven cuisine groups. That brings our target catalogue to **147 recipes** across meals, desserts, snacks and drinks.

Drinks fit the farm-to-table idea particularly well. Something as familiar as æblemost requires growing apples, harvesting, crushing, pressing and bottling. Hyldeblomstsaft involves foraging, infusion, straining and preparing a concentrate.

We can also introduce new techniques such as **juice pressing, infusion, brewing, filtration, syrup making and carbonation**.

### 8. New processing methods

Drinks help justify several reusable mechanics.

| Process | Equipment | Products |
|---|---|---|
| Crushing and pressing | Fruit crusher, juice press | Æblemost, grape juice, citrus juice |
| Infusion | Pot, infusion jar | Elderflower extract, herbal teas |
| Brewing | Kettle, brewing vessel | Tea, coffee, roasted barley tea |
| Straining | Strainer / filter | Clear juice, syrups, tea |
| Concentration | Cooking pot | Fruit syrup, cordial concentrates |
| Grinding and blending | Mill, blender | Horchata, smoothies, chocolate drinks |
| Chilling | Cold storage / ice | Cold drinks and desserts |
| Carbonation | Carbonator | Sparkling water, lemonade |
| Controlled enzymatic processing | Fermentation vessel | Koji amazake |
| Bottling | Bottle filling station | Finished drinks and concentrates |

I'd keep ice and cooling fairly simple. Cooling should matter for certain recipes, with different temperatures producing different results, just like our other cooking processes.

### 9. Concentrates could become a useful ingredient family

I particularly like the distinction between drinks made directly from fruit and drinks made from concentrates.

**Pressed juice**

Concentrates should produce several servings when diluted, making them more efficient to store.

A single bottle of elderflower concentrate could also be used in desserts, glazes or other recipes.

This makes concentrate automation attractive for the cabin.

### 10. Drinks and gameplay buffs

I'd let drinks complement food through a small separate buff category.

| Drink type | Suggested benefit |
|---|---|
| Fresh juices | Short recovery or endurance |
| Fruit cordials | Small, immediate energy benefit |
| Tea and coffee | Focus or perception |
| Warm spiced drinks | Warmth or resilience |
| Rich milk/chocolate drinks | Longer-lasting minor endurance |
| Elaborate drinks | Better potency or duration through their processing |

I'd avoid introducing a thirst meter. Drinks should be something players want to prepare because they're useful.

A meal buff and a drink buff could coexist, with only one main drink effect active at a time.

### 12. Automation and the drinks pantry

I'd make the first useful drink automation recipes:

- Press apples into æblemost.
- Maintain elderflower or blackcurrant concentrate.
- Brew and bottle iced tea.
- Prepare fruit juice in batches.
- Keep a supply of bottled drinks in storage.

Later, the cabin could handle a more complete production chain, including harvesting fruit, preparing concentrates, filling bottles and maintaining pantry stock targets.

**I'd particularly prioritise æblemost, hyldeblomstsaft, horchata, chicha morada, mugicha, matcha, cha yen and champurrado.** They cover a useful range of equipment and techniques, and they demonstrate that drinks can be as interesting to produce as meals.

That gives us 35 candidate drinks with almost no emphasis on alcohol, while expanding the usefulness of farming, fruit trees, milling, drying, brewing and temperature control.
